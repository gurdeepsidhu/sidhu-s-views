How to Crochet a 3D Daisy Granny Square: Step-by-Step Tutorial & Pattern

A mindful, beginner-friendly guide to crocheting a dimensional floral motif with 12 textured popcorn petals, crisp sage corners, and clean blocking techniques.

Sidhu's Views • November 11, 2026 • 10 min read • Handmade & Crafts
Language / भाषा: 🇬🇧 English Edition | 🇮🇳 हिंदी में पढ़ें →

"There is a quiet, meditative magic in holding a wooden crochet hook on a brisk autumn evening. With every loop pulled through, a flat circle of yarn gently awakens into a sculpted blooming flower."

Standard granny squares made purely of flat double-crochet clusters are lovely, but they often lack texture. The 3D Pop-Up Daisy Granny Square takes classic granny geometry and elevates it into botanical art. Instead of lying flat, twelve distinct daisy petals cup forward using dimensional Popcorn Stitches, giving the motif tangible depth, warmth, and vintage cottagecore charm.

Whether you are making a single square to serve as a tea mug rug, or creating twenty squares for a patchwork tote bag or winter lap blanket, this tutorial provides exact stitch counts, color transition guidance, and crisp squaring instructions.

Materials, Color Palette & Pattern Specifications

Skill Level Confident Beginner to Intermediate (Familiarity with double crochet and magic ring recommended).
Finished Size 4.5 inches × 4.5 inches (11.5 cm × 11.5 cm) square after wet blocking.
Yarn Type 100% Mercerized Cotton or Cotton-Acrylic Blend (DK / Light Worsted weight, approx. 120m per 50g). Cotton holds stitch definition crisply without fuzzing.
Hook Size 4.0 mm (US G/6) wooden or aluminum hook.
Notions Yarn/tapestry needle, sharp embroidery scissors, blocking board, and rust-proof T-pins.
Terminology US Standard Crochet Terms.

The Curated Botanical Color Palette

For a soothing, earth-toned aesthetic that suits slow living spaces, we use four harmonious shades:

Color A: Golden Ochre (~8 meters) - Flower Center Color B: Natural Cream (~16 meters) - 3D Petals Color C: Muted Sage Green (~12 meters) - Squaring Round Color D: Warm Oatmeal Beige (~16 meters) - Outer Frame

Stitch Glossary & The 3D Popcorn Technique

This pattern uses standard crochet stitches along with one specialty dimensional stitch:

  • ch — Chain
  • sl st — Slip Stitch
  • sc — Single Crochet
  • hdc — Half Double Crochet
  • dc — Double Crochet
  • tr — Treble Crochet (yarn over twice, insert hook, pull up loop, [yo, pull through 2] 3 times)
  • sp — Space / Chain Space

How to Work the 3D Popcorn Stitch (pc)

  1. Work 5 double crochets (dc) into the designated stitch or space.
  2. Carefully release the working loop from your hook (leave it loose so it doesn't unravel).
  3. Insert your hook from front to back through the top two loops of the very first double crochet of the 5-dc group.
  4. Grab the loose working loop with your hook and pull it smoothly through the first stitch.
  5. Chain 1 firmly to lock the popcorn shut.

Result: The 5 stitches fold inward into a plump, rounded petal that physically arches forward off the square!

Step-by-Step Tutorial & Pattern Instructions

Step 1 • Round 1

The Golden Ochre Center Button

Yarn: Color A (Golden Ochre)

  1. Start with a Magic Ring (alternatively: ch 4, sl st to first ch to form a ring).
  2. Ch 2 (does not count as a stitch; this acts as height transition).
  3. Work 12 double crochets (dc) directly into the magic ring.
  4. Gently but firmly pull the yarn tail to close the center hole completely.
  5. Join with a sl st into the top of the very first double crochet (not into the starting ch-2).
  6. Fasten off Color A and weave in the center tail securely.

Round 1 Stitch Count: Exactly 12 double crochets.

Step 2 • Round 2

The 3D Popcorn Petals in Cream

Yarn: Color B (Natural Cream)

  1. Join Color B with a standing slip stitch in any stitch around the circle.
  2. First Petal: Ch 3 (counts as first dc), work 4 dc into the same stitch (5 dc total). Drop loop, insert hook into 3rd chain of starting ch-3 from front to back, pull loop through, ch 1 to close. Ch 2 for space.
  3. Remaining 11 Petals: In each of the next 11 stitches around, work: 1 Popcorn Stitch, followed by ch 2.
  4. Join with a sl st into the chain-1 closing stitch at the back of the first popcorn petal.
  5. Fasten off Color B.

Round 2 Stitch Count: Exactly 12 Popcorn Petals and 12 ch-2 spaces.

Step 3 • Round 3

Squaring the Circle in Muted Sage Green

Yarn: Color C (Muted Sage Green)

The Math: We have 12 chain-2 spaces around the flower. To form a square, 4 spaces become corners, and the remaining 8 spaces form 4 straight sides (2 spaces per side). The height difference between tall Treble Crochets at the corners and Double Crochets along the sides flattens the circle into a true square.

  1. Join Color C in any ch-2 sp between petals.
  2. First Corner: Ch 4 (counts as first tr), work 2 tr into same space, ch 2, work 3 tr into same space → (3 tr, ch 2, 3 tr).
  3. First Side: Work 3 dc into the next ch-2 space. Work 3 dc into the next ch-2 space.
  4. Second Corner: Work (3 tr, ch 2, 3 tr) into the next ch-2 space.
  5. Second Side: Work 3 dc in next space, 3 dc in next space.
  6. Repeat steps 4 & 5 two more times to complete the 3rd and 4th corners and sides.
  7. Join with a sl st to the 4th chain of the starting ch-4.
  8. Fasten off Color C.

Round 3 Stitch Count: 4 Corners (each with 6 tr & ch-2 sp) + 8 side clusters (3 dc each). Total 48 stitches.

Step 4 • Rounds 4 & 5

The Oatmeal Frame & Clean Border

Yarn: Color D (Warm Oatmeal Beige)

Round 4 (Solid Frame):

  1. Join Color D in any corner ch-2 sp.
  2. Corner: Ch 3 (counts as dc), work (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in corner space.
  3. Side: Work 3 dc into each of the 3 spaces between clusters along the side.
  4. Repeat [Corner, Side] around all 4 sides.
  5. Join with sl st into top of starting ch-3.

Round 5 (Clean Edging):

  1. Ch 1 (does not count as st), work 1 sc into each dc stitch across the sides.
  2. In each of the 4 corner ch-2 spaces, work: (1 sc, ch 2, 1 sc).
  3. Join with an invisible join or slip stitch to first sc. Fasten off and weave in all yarn ends neatly at the back.

Finished Dimensions before blocking: approx. 4.25 inches square.

The Essential Wet-Blocking Technique

Many beginners skip blocking, thinking it is an optional chore. For a 3D floral granny square, however, blocking is the difference between a handmade craft and an heirloom masterpiece:

  1. Dampen the Square: Submerge your finished square in lukewarm water with a drop of wool wash for 5 minutes, then gently press the water out between two clean towels (never wring or twist). Alternatively, spritz liberally with a fine-mist spray bottle.
  2. Pin to Grid: Lay the square onto a foam blocking mat. Using stainless steel T-pins, pin all four corners at exact 90-degree angles, gently stretching it to precisely 4.5 inches square. Place 2 to 3 additional pins along each straight edge.
  3. Gently Fluff the Petals: While damp, use your fingertips to gently shape each of the 12 cream popcorn petals upward so they cup forward uniformly.
  4. Let Dry Completely: Allow the square to dry undisturbed for 12 to 18 hours. When you remove the pins, the cotton yarn will retain its crisp geometric shape permanently.

Creative Projects for Your 3D Daisy Squares

Once you have mastered this single square, you hold the building block for countless cozy winter home projects:

Botanical Tea Mug Rug

Use a single 4.5" blocked square as an elegant, heat-resistant coaster for your morning tea or ceramic mugs.

Daisy Meadow Cushion

Join 16 squares (4 × 4 grid) for a stunning 18-inch vintage sofa pillow cover with breathable cotton backing.

Market Tote Bag

Assemble 13 squares into the classic French market tote construction with sturdy cotton-strap handles.

Winter Lap Blanket

Join 35 to 48 squares with continuous join-as-you-go borders for a breathtaking heirloom winter throw.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my popcorn petals curling backwards instead of popping forward?
When closing the popcorn stitch, make sure you insert your hook into the first double crochet from front to back. Pulling the loop through from the back pushes the petal bulk forward, giving it that crisp 3D dimensional cup shape. If you insert hook from back to front, the petal will invert into a dent!
What is the best yarn for crocheting 3D daisy squares?
100% mercerized cotton or a smooth 50/50 cotton-acrylic blend in DK or Worsted weight is ideal. Cotton provides crisp stitch definition, keeps petals from sagging, and blocks out into flat, perfectly square edges. Fuzzy acrylics tend to blur the petal outlines.
How do I turn 12 round daisy petals into a 4-sided square?
The 12 chain-2 spaces are divided evenly: 4 spaces become corners using tall Treble Crochet clusters (3 tr, ch 2, 3 tr), while the remaining 8 spaces form the 4 flat sides using pairs of Double Crochet clusters (3 dc). The height difference automatically squares the circle.
How should I join multiple daisy granny squares together?
The mattress stitch with a tapestry needle creates a completely invisible, flat seam. Alternatively, a slip-stitch join on the wrong side creates a neat, sturdy ridge seam ideal for tote bags and cushion covers.
