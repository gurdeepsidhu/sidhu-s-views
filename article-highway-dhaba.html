Notes from a Highway Dhaba: Tea, Parathas & Life on the Open Road

There is a simple joy in pulling over under a big shady neem tree, resting your feet on a wooden cot, and drinking hot ginger tea while watching the world drive by.

By Gurdeep Sidhu • September 30, 2026 • 6 min read

If you have ever been on a long car journey in India, you know the exact feeling. It usually happens around three o’clock in the afternoon. The sun is shining bright, your eyes feel heavy from looking at the straight road, and your legs feel completely stiff. Your car AC is running fine, but what your mind really wants is a cool shade, a glass of water, and a hot cup of tea.

And then, just like magic, you see a colourful wooden sign by the side of the road that says "PUNJABI DHABA". It is built right under a huge, green neem tree. Outside, there are clean wooden cots (charpais) lined up neatly under thatched roofs with warm yellow lights. You take a quick turn, park your car in the shade, and step out. Instantly, all your tiredness vanishes into the fresh country breeze.

1. Why a Roadside Cot feels Better than a Five-Star Bed

The moment you sit down on a woven jute charpai, your back feels so relaxed. The ropes are stretched just right—not too hard and not too soft. You take off your shoes, stretch your legs out, and look up at the green neem leaves swaying softly in the wind. You cannot help but smile and think: why did fancy hotels ever replace these wonderful cots with heavy modern sofas?

Before you even look around, a young waiter boy walks over to your cot with a big, friendly smile. He carries a shiny metal jug of cold water and four metal glasses. He sets them down with a small clink, pours out water smoothly without spilling a single drop, and wipes his hands with a clean towel on his shoulder.

2. The Boy with No Menu and a Superfast Memory

There are no big printed books or fancy menu cards at Punjabi Dhaba. The waiter boy simply stands by your cot, takes a quick breath, and speaks like a fast radio sports reporter: "Aloo paratha, paneer paratha, dal makhani, kadai paneer, tandoori roti, and fresh ginger kulhad chai, Sirji!"

He says it so fast and with so much confidence that you don't even need time to think. You laugh and say, "Bring two hot aloo parathas with white butter, and two cups of ginger tea!" He gives a quick nod and rushes off towards the kitchen fire.

"A highway dhaba does not care about fancy rules. It gives you hot food, honest smiles, and a peaceful seat under the sky."

3. Hot Tandoori Parathas & The Secret of White Butter

If you walk near the kitchen, you can see the cook working his magic. He stands near a red-hot clay oven (tandoor). He takes a round ball of wheat dough, fills it with spiced mashed potatoes, rolls it flat, and slaps it directly onto the inner wall of the oven with his hand. You hear a loud, tasty sizzle!

Two minutes later, he pulls out a hot, golden paratha with crisp brown edges. He puts it on a clean plate, reaches into a cold clay jar, and puts a big scoop of fresh white homemade butter (makhan) right in the center. The butter melts instantly, turning into a shiny golden river over the hot bread.

He serves it with fresh green mint chutney and a small bowl of spicy mango pickle. Eating a hot buttered paratha out in the open air, with the cool afternoon wind on your face, is pure happiness. No expensive restaurant meal can ever beat this simplicity.

4. Clay Cup Chai: The Real Medicine for Highway Tiredness

While the parathas fill your stomach, the tea brings your mind back to life. Making tea here is a slow and happy art. The tea maker crushes fresh ginger root and green cardamom with a small iron tool. He throws them into a big copper pot boiling over a steady flame, along with tea leaves, water, and fresh thick milk.

He lets it boil till it turns dark and fragrant. Then he lifts the pot high in the air and pours the tea into small brown clay cups called kulhads. When you hold the clay cup, the warmth feels so good in your hands. The clay cup gives the tea a sweet, earthly smell—just like the smell of rain dropping on dry soil.

As you take your first sip—hot, sweet, and rich with ginger—all your highway head-ache and fatigue disappear instantly!

5. Truck Drivers, Farmers & Stories Over Tea

The best thing about a dhaba is that anyone can sit here and talk. A dhaba does not care if you drive an expensive car or a rusty old tractor. Everyone sits on the same wooden cots and drinks the same tea.

On the cot next to yours, a friendly truck driver named Gurmail Singh sits with his tea cup. He has a white beard and a bright orange turban. He has been driving his big truck for three days straight from Himachal down to Mumbai. He smiles and tells you funny stories about driving up narrow mountain roads where monkeys try to snatch snacks from open truck windows!

On another cot, an old village farmer rests his bicycle and predicts the evening weather by looking at the clouds. For half an hour, nobody touches their mobile phone. Nobody is checking emails. Everyone is just relaxed, sipping tea, laughing, and enjoying the breeze.

6. Back on the Road with a Full Belly and a Happy Heart

By the time you finish your second cup of tea, the sun starts setting behind the green fields. The sky turns a beautiful shade of orange and pink, and small yellow fairy lights turn on all around Punjabi Dhaba.

Your legs feel light, your stomach is full of good food, and you feel completely refreshed. You pay the small bill, thank the smiling waiter boy, and walk back to your car. As you turn the key and drive back onto the smooth highway, you look back in your mirror one last time.

The cook is already making the next hot paratha, tea is boiling in the pot, and another tired driver is pulling up to rest under the neem tree. The highway carries on fast, but the roadside dhaba reminds us of one sweet truth: the best part of any journey is taking a break to enjoy simple things.
