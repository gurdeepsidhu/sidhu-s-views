The Great Sunday Samosa Mystery: How My Brother Almost Caused a Family Riot

A sweet, hilarious memory of an Indian Sunday morning, a missing bag of samosas, angry eyes at the door, and an unbothered brother who only cared about lunch.

By Gurdeep Sidhu • September 20, 2026 • 7 min read • Funny Nostalgia

  2. Scene 2: The Agonizing Two-Hour Wait (10:00 AM - 12:00 PM)
  3. Scene 3: The Grand Arrival & The Empty Hands (12:30 PM)
  4. Scene 4: The Thunder of Mother & The Innocent Question
  5. Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest Memories

In an Indian household, a Sunday morning is not just a day of the week—it is a sacred festival of food, laziness, and high drama.

And in our home, no Sunday morning was complete without the supreme king of all breakfast snacks: **hot, golden, extra-crispy Samosas from Ramu Halwai's corner shop**.

The routine was always the same. Father would sit on the veranda reading the morning newspaper, Mother would be boiling a big pot of ginger tea, and my sister and I would sit near the window with mouths watering, counting down the minutes until breakfast.

"There is no anger in the world quite like the anger of a hungry sibling whose Sunday samosas have disappeared into thin air."

Scene 1: The Sacred Sunday Morning Order (9:00 AM)

At exactly nine o'clock, Mother called my thirteen-year-old brother, Raju, into the kitchen. She handed him a crisp 50-rupee note with strict, military-grade instructions:

"Listen to me carefully, Raju," Mother warned, holding him by the shoulder. "Go straight to Ramu Halwai's shop. Tell him to pack six hot samosas. Make sure he adds extra green mint chutney and sweet tamarind chutney. Do NOT talk to your friends. Do NOT stop at the playground. Come straight home in ten minutes!"

Raju nodded like a brave soldier heading off on a national mission. My sister and I stood near the front gate, waving at him like he was an astronaut blasting off to the moon.

"Bring the super crispy ones!" my sister yelled after him. "And don't spill the chutney!" Raju gave us a thumb-up, slipped the 50-rupee note into his pocket, and trotted down the dusty lane.

Scene 2: The Agonizing Two-Hour Wait (10:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

Ten minutes passed... Fifteen minutes passed... Half an hour passed.

By 10:30 AM, our ginger tea had gone cold. My stomach began making noises like thunder over the mountains of Malgudi. My sister and I sat on the veranda steps, staring down the empty lane like abandoned puppies.

By 11:30 AM, Mother was pacing up and down the corridor with a wooden cooking spoon in her hand.

The Rising Tension at Home

"Where on earth is this boy?!" Mother muttered, checking the wall clock every forty seconds. "Did he fall into the halwai's giant oil pan? Did aliens kidnap him for fifty rupees? Just let him come home... I will show him what happens when you make this family wait for breakfast!"

My sister cracked her knuckles with a dangerous grin. "If he ate even half a samosa without us," she whispered, her eyes turning bright red with fury, "I am going to land a double-fisted punch straight on his head!"

Scene 3: The Grand Arrival & The Empty Hands (12:30 PM)

High noon arrived. The sun was burning hot at 12:30 PM. The Sunday breakfast dream was completely dead. We were no longer just hungry; we were fierce warriors ready for battle.

Suddenly, a cheerful, careless whistle echoed from down the street.

We turned around—and there was Raju.

He strolled into the compound with a peaceful, easy-going smile on his face, like a philosopher who had just returned from ten years of meditation in the Himalayas. His knees were covered in brown dirt, his shirt collar was unbuttoned, his hair looked like a crow's nest after a storm, and in his right hand, he was swinging a **broken wooden cricket bat**.

And in his left hand? **NOTHING.**

No newspaper bag. No samosas. Not even a single drop of green chutney!

Scene 4: The Thunder of Mother & The Innocent Question

My sister and I stood up instantly, our eyes bulging out with pure rage. We looked like two hungry tigers ready to pounce on him and eat him alive right there on the veranda mat!

Before we could land our punches, Mother burst out of the doorway like a lightning bolt. Her scolding shook the roof tiles:

"RAJU! Where are the samosas?! Where is the fifty-rupee note?! Do you know what time it is?! The whole house has been waiting since nine in the morning! Were you playing gully cricket with your useless friends while your family starved at home?!"

Mother scolded him continuously for three solid minutes without stopping to breathe. It was a masterclass in Indian motherly fury.

And how did Raju react? He stood there quietly, blinking his big round eyes like an innocent owl. He didn't run, he didn't cry, and he didn't apologize. He simply waited patiently until Mother finally stopped to catch her breath.

When the storm quieted down for just one second, Raju wiped the sweat off his dusty forehead, looked straight into the kitchen, and asked in the sweetest, most innocent voice:

The Legendary Question

"Mummy, what is in the lunch today? I am very hungry. Can you serve me rice quickly?"

My sister’s jaw literally dropped to the floor. My hands froze in mid-air. Mother stared at him in utter disbelief, her mouth wide open, completely frozen in shock.

He had forgotten the samosas, lost the money, played cricket for three hours, made the whole house starve, and now he was asking for lunch as if he were the guest of honor at a royal banquet!

After five seconds of total silence, Mother’s angry face slowly collapsed. She tried to stay strict, but a small smile broke out on her lips, and within seconds, the whole veranda erupted into uncontrollable laughter.

Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest Memories

We never got our Sunday samosas that day. We ended up eating simple dal-rice for lunch while listening to Raju explain how he hit a sixer that broke Gupta ji's window glass down the street.

Looking back today, years after those childhood days have passed, I realize that those lost samosas were never really about the food. They were about the crazy, chaotic, beautiful warmth of an Indian family Sunday—where anger turns into laughter in a split second, and where an innocent brother's silly question can turn a ruined morning into a lifetime memory.
