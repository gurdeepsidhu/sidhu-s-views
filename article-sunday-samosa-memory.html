The Great Sunday Samosa Mystery: How My Brother Almost Caused a Family Riot

A sweet, hilarious memory of 1990s childhood: when there were no mobile phones, fast food wasn't a daily trend, and an unbothered brother forgot the samosas and innocently asked for lunch.

By Gurdeep Sidhu • September 20, 2026 • 7 min read • 90s Nostalgia
Language: English | 🇮🇳 हिंदी (Hinglish) →

  2. Scene 2: The Agonizing Two-Hour Wait Without Mobile Phones (10:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

  4. Scene 4: Mother's Thunder & The Legendary Question
  5. Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest 90s Memories

Back in the 1990s, childhood had a completely different rhythm. There were no mobile phones, no screens to glue our eyes to, and no endless scrolling. Brothers and sisters actually spent real time together—inventing games, fighting over cricket bats, and chatting on the veranda.

And in those 90s days, fast food was definitely not a daily affair, nor was it any kind of trendy lifestyle. Daily outside food was strictly forbidden in our home. Getting something from outside—especially hot, golden, crispy samosas from the corner halwai—was never a fixed weekly rule or routine. It was an extremely rare, special treat that happened only on very few occasions.

But precisely because it happened so rarely, on the occasional Sunday morning when Mother finally agreed to send for samosas, our excitement hit the roof! The entire house was filled with an electric buzz of joy. Mother was boiling a fragrant pot of ginger tea in the kitchen, while my sister and I were perched by the window with mouths watering, practically vibrating with excitement and counting down the seconds.

"In the 90s, outside snacks weren't a routine—they were a rare festival. And when a missing bag of samosas tested that excitement, our family drama reached peak comedy."

Scene 1: The Rare 90s Sunday Morning Mission (9:00 AM)

At exactly nine o'clock, Mother called my thirteen-year-old brother, Raju, into the kitchen. Handing him a crisp 50-rupee note, she gave him strict, military-grade instructions:

"Listen to me carefully, Raju," Mother warned, looking straight into his eyes. "Go straight to the halwai shop. Tell him to pack six hot samosas. Make sure he adds extra green mint chutney and sweet tamarind chutney. Do NOT talk to your friends. Do NOT stop at the playground. Come straight back home in ten minutes!"

Raju nodded solemnly, looking like a brave commando embarking on a national mission. My sister and I stood by the front gate, waving him off like an astronaut launching into orbit.

"Bring the extra crispy ones!" my sister yelled after him. "And don't spill the chutney!" Raju gave us a confident thumb-up, stuffed the 50-rupee note deep into his pocket, and trotted down the dusty street.

Scene 2: The Agonizing Two-Hour Wait Without Mobile Phones (10:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

Ten minutes passed... Twenty minutes passed... An hour passed.

Remember, this was the 1990s—there were no mobile phones! You couldn't ring him up, you couldn't send a quick message, and you couldn't track his location. All you could do was wait, pace the floor, and stare down the empty road.

By 10:30 AM, our ginger tea had gone cold. My stomach was grumbling loudly like thunder over the hills. My sister and I sat on the veranda steps, peering into the distance like stranded sailors waiting for a rescue ship.

By 11:30 AM, Mother was pacing up and down the corridor with a wooden cooking spoon in her hand, glancing at the wall clock every thirty seconds.

The Rising 90s Drama at Home

"Where on earth is this boy?!" Mother muttered, checking the clock again. "Did he fall into the halwai's giant oil kadhai? Did someone kidnap him for fifty rupees? Just let him step through that gate... I will teach him what happens when you make the whole house starve for breakfast!"

My sister cracked her knuckles with a murderous grin. "If he ate even half a samosa by himself," she whispered, her eyes narrowing with vengeance, "I am going to land a double-fisted punch straight on his head the second he walks in!"

Scene 3: The Grand Arrival & The Empty Hands (12:30 PM)

High noon arrived. The afternoon sun was blazing at 12:30 PM. The Sunday breakfast dream was officially dead and buried. We were no longer just hungry children; we were fierce warriors waiting in ambush.

Suddenly, a cheerful, careless whistle echoed down the lane.

We turned around—and there was Raju.

He strolled through the front gate with a serene, peaceful smile, looking like a monk who had just attained enlightenment after ten years of meditation in the mountains. His knees were caked in dry mud, his shirt was unbuttoned at the collar, his hair looked like a crow's nest after a cyclone, and in his right hand, he was casually swinging a broken wooden cricket bat.

And in his left hand? NOTHING.

No newspaper parcel. No paper bag. Not a single samosa. Not even a microscopic speck of mint chutney!

Scene 4: Mother's Thunder & The Legendary Question

My sister and I leapt up instantly, ready to tackle him right onto the veranda mat. But before our fists could fly, Mother stormed out of the kitchen like a lightning strike. Her voice rattled the door frames:

"RAJU! Where are the samosas?! Where is the fifty-rupee note?! Look at the clock! The entire house has been starving since nine in the morning! Were you playing gully cricket with your friends all this time while everyone starved?!"

Mother unleashed a breathless, three-minute masterclass in Indian motherly fury.

And how did Raju react? He stood there perfectly still, blinking his big round eyes like an innocent owl. He didn't run, he didn't cry, and he didn't offer a single excuse. He simply waited patiently until Mother finally stopped to catch her breath.

When the storm paused for just one quiet second, Raju casually wiped the sweat off his dusty forehead, looked straight toward the kitchen, and asked in the sweetest, most innocent voice:

The Legendary Question

"Mummy, what is in the lunch today? I am very hungry. Can you serve me rice quickly?"

My sister’s jaw dropped to the floor. My fists froze in mid-air. Mother stared at him in utter disbelief, completely paralyzed by the sheer audacity of that innocent question.

He had forgotten the samosas, lost the money, played cricket for three hours, made his mother and sisters starve half the day, and now he was asking for lunch as if he were an honored royal guest arriving for a banquet!

After five seconds of stunned silence, Mother’s fierce expression began to crack. She bit her lip trying to stay strict, but a giggle broke through—and within seconds, the entire veranda exploded into uncontrollable laughter.

Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest 90s Memories

We never got our samosas that day. We sat together and ate simple dal-rice for lunch, while Raju enthusiastically narrated how he smashed a massive sixer that broke a neighbor's windowpane down the street.

Looking back today at those 1990s childhood days, I realize that those lost samosas were never about the food at all. They were about the simplicity of a screen-free life—an era when fast food was rare, sibling bonds were forged in dust and laughter, and where an innocent brother's silly question could turn a ruined morning into a story told with smiles for decades.
