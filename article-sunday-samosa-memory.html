The Great Sunday Samosa Incident of the 90s

A sweet, hilarious memory of 1990s childhood: when there were no mobile phones, fast food wasn't a daily trend, and an unbothered brother forgot the samosas and innocently asked for lunch.

By Gurdeep Sidhu • September 20, 2026 • 7 min read • 90s Nostalgia
Language: English | 🇮🇳 हिंदी (Hinglish) →

  4. Scene 4: Mother's Thunder & The Legendary Question
  5. Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest 90s Memories

Back in the 1990s, childhood had a completely different rhythm. There were no mobile phones, no screens to glue our eyes to, and no endless scrolling. Brothers and sisters actually spent real time together—inventing games, fighting over cricket bats, and chatting on the veranda.

And in those 90s days, fast food was definitely not a daily affair, nor was it any kind of trendy lifestyle. Daily outside food was strictly forbidden in our home. Getting something from outside—especially hot, golden, crispy samosas from the corner halwai—was never a fixed weekly rule or routine. It was an extremely rare, special treat that happened only on very few occasions.

But precisely because it happened so rarely, on the occasional Sunday morning when Mother finally agreed to send for samosas, our excitement hit the roof! The entire house was filled with an electric buzz of joy. Mother was boiling a fragrant pot of ginger tea in the kitchen, while my sister and I were perched by the window with mouths watering, practically vibrating with excitement and counting down the seconds.

"In the 90s, outside snacks weren't a routine—they were a rare festival. And when a missing bag of samosas tested that excitement, our family drama reached peak comedy."

Scene 1: The Rare 90s Sunday Morning Mission (9:00 AM)

At exactly nine o'clock, Mother called my thirteen-year-old brother, Raju, into the kitchen. Handing him a crisp 50-rupee note, she gave him strict, clear instructions:

"Listen to me carefully, Raju," Mother said, looking him in the eye. "Go straight to the halwai shop. Tell him to pack eight hot samosas—two for each of the four of us. Make sure he packs extra green mint chutney and sweet tamarind chutney. Don't get distracted on the way, don't stop anywhere, and come straight back in ten minutes!"

Raju nodded with complete, earnest seriousness. My sister and I stood by the front gate, waving him off eagerly.

"Get the super crispy ones!" my sister called after him. "And don't drop the chutney!" Raju gave us a quick thumbs-up, tucked the 50-rupee note into his pocket, and ran down the lane.

Scene 2: The Agonizing Two-Hour Wait Without Mobile Phones (10:00 AM - 12:00 PM)

Ten minutes passed... Twenty minutes passed... Half an hour passed.

Remember, this was the 1990s—there were no mobile phones! We had no way to call him, and no one at home had any clue what was taking so long. Was there a massive Sunday morning queue at the halwai? Had the first batch run out, with a fresh batch still frying?

By 10:30 AM, our pot of ginger tea had gone completely cold. My sister and I sat on the veranda steps, hungry and restless, staring down the quiet street like little puppies waiting for someone to come home.

By 11:30 AM, Mother was checking the wall clock every couple of minutes, genuinely puzzled and impatient:

Growing Impatience at Home

"Where on earth has Raju gotten stuck?" Mother sighed, looking out the doorway. "Did he forget his way back? The tea is completely ruined, and it's already time to start preparing lunch!"

My sister rubbed her rumbling stomach with an annoyed pout. "If he got distracted and made us wait this long," she grumbled, "I'm definitely not sharing my portion of the sweet chutney with him!"

Scene 3: The Grand Arrival & The Empty Hands (12:30 PM)

High noon arrived. The clock struck 12:30 PM. The morning breakfast dream was long gone, replaced by pure bewilderment.

Suddenly, a cheerful, careless whistle echoed down the lane.

We looked up—and there was Raju!

He strolled through the front gate with a serene, peaceful smile, looking completely calm and relaxed. His knees were covered in dry dust, his collar unbuttoned, his hair ruffled by the wind, and in his right hand, he was casually swinging a broken wooden cricket bat.

And in his other hand? NOTHING.

No newspaper parcel. No paper bag. Not a single samosa, and not a single drop of chutney!

In that split second, the whole mystery solved itself: the neighborhood boys had called him for a quick gully cricket game on his way, and he had gotten so lost in the match that he had forgotten time, samosas, and home altogether!

Scene 4: Mother's Scolding & The Legendary Question

My sister and I stood there frozen in disbelief. Mother stepped out of the kitchen, staring at his dusty clothes and broken bat in total astonishment:

"RAJU! Where are the samosas?! Where is the fifty-rupee note?! Look at the time—it's half past twelve! We have all been waiting since nine in the morning! Have you been playing cricket with your friends all this time?!"

Mother scolded him with that breathless mix of parental exasperation, relief, and utter disbelief that only Indian mothers can deliver.

And how did Raju react? He stood there quietly, blinking his big round eyes with absolute innocence. He didn't make up wild excuses, and he didn't panic. He simply waited patiently for Mother to finish.

When the scolding paused for breath, Raju casually wiped the sweat from his dusty forehead, looked straight into the kitchen, and asked in the sweetest, most innocent voice:

The Legendary Question

"Mummy, what is in the lunch today? I am very hungry. Can you serve me rice quickly?"

My sister's jaw dropped. My hands fell to my sides. Mother stared at him, utterly speechless.

He had forgotten breakfast, played three hours of cricket, lost the money, made the whole house wait half the day, and now he was innocently asking for lunch as if nothing out of the ordinary had happened!

After a brief, stunned silence, Mother's stern look completely dissolved. She tried her best to hold a serious face, but a smile broke through—and within seconds, the entire veranda erupted into helpless, warm laughter.

Scene 5: Why Lost Samosas Become the Sweetest 90s Memories

We never got our samosas that day. We all sat together and ate simple, warm dal-rice for lunch, while Raju enthusiastically narrated how his towering six had broken Gupta ji's windowpane down the street and cracked his cricket bat.

Looking back today at those 1990s childhood days, I smile thinking about how pure, simple, and unhurried life was. An era without mobile phones, where small treats were cherished, where a little brother's innocent question could melt away all exasperation, and where a lost bag of samosas became a story our family laughs about even decades later.
